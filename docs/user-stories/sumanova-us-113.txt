=========================================================================================
sumanova-us-113 · Real skin tones and skin details — my exact tone, undertone and marks
=========================================================================================

USER STORY
  As a SUMANOVA user, I want my avatar's skin to be my real colour — the right depth and
  the right undertone (cool, neutral, warm or olive) — and to carry the things that make
  my skin mine (freckles, moles, a beauty spot, vitiligo, a birthmark, a scar, a rosy
  flush), so that my avatar truly looks like me and represents every skin respectfully,
  far beyond Bitmoji's flat tone swatches.

WHY (what build-103 does today)
  - Skin is one hex (Look.skin) picked from 100 HSL swatches (look/palettes.ts SKIN:
    10 depth bands × 10 steps, hue 22±11°). There is no undertone choice: olive and cool
    pink-beige skins can't be made.
  - Rendered faces read orange: SkinMat (render/Face.tsx) adds a strong warm sheen
    (#ffb8a0 / #e0926a at sheen 0.42–1.0) under a warm key light, a warm rim light and a
    brown hemisphere ground (AvatarStage.tsx), on top of react-three-fiber's default
    ACES tone mapping. The swatch the user taps does not match what they see.
  - selfie/generate.ts darkens the measured skin by a fixed 0.92 to "compensate" — a
    guess, not a calibration.
  - No skin marks of any kind; head and body meshes have no UVs (sculpted fields), so
    texture decals can't simply be painted on.

1) COLOUR ACCURACY (skin only, under today's studio light)
   a. Measure first: in the harness render a front face for 20 tones (2 per depth band)
      and sample the lit cheek (a 12×12 px patch facing the camera). Compute CIE ΔE2000
      between the sampled colour and the swatch shown in the picker. Record the baseline.
   b. Fix in the skin material, not in the lights: lower and neutralise the sheen
      (sheenColor ≈ mix(skin, white) instead of fixed salmon/orange; sheen ≤ 0.35 for light,
      ≤ 0.55 for deep skin), keep a gentle clearcoat glow, and add a small "calibration"
      correction (per depth band, computed once, stored as a constant table) so the lit
      cheek mid-tone matches the swatch. Deep skins must keep their rich glow (no grey /
      ashy look).
   c. The picker swatches show the CALIBRATED displayed colour (what the cheek looks like),
      so what you tap is what you get.
   d. Make this an automated check: e2e/skin-accuracy.e2e.cjs renders the 20 tones and
      fails when any ΔE2000 ≥ 6. us-186 must keep this passing when it changes lighting.
   e. Remove the fixed ×0.92 in selfie/generate.ts skinForAvatar(); the selfie now maps
      straight to the calibrated palette (step 5).

2) DEPTH + UNDERTONE COLOUR MODEL — look/palettes.ts + look/config.ts
   a. New palette SKIN2: 24 depth steps (very fair → very deep, evenly spaced in CIE L*)
      × 4 undertones = 96 tones, all original, generated in OKLCH/LCh:
        cool    – pink / rosy (lower hue, a* up)
        neutral – balanced
        warm    – golden / peach (higher hue, b* up)
        olive   – green-grey cast (lower chroma, hue toward yellow-green)
      Keep the old SKIN array (100) untouched: saved looks use those hexes.
   b. Look gets an optional `skinSel?: { depth: number; under: 'cool'|'neutral'|'warm'|'olive'; fine?: { h: number; l: number } }`.
      Look.skin (hex) stays the single source used by every renderer — skinSel only
      restores the picker. `fine` is a small nudge (hue ±6°, lightness ±4 L*) for an exact
      match. normalizeLook validates/clamps skinSel; old looks (no skinSel) keep their hex
      and the picker highlights the nearest depth/undertone.
   c. Derived tones follow the final skin: lipTone(), nailTone(), the inner-eye corner, ear
      shading and blush defaults use the undertone (cool → rosier lips, olive → muted).

3) SKIN LAYERS (shared system) — new render/skinLayers.ts + SkinMat
   a. SkinMat gets an optional `layers` prop and compiles ONE extended material
      (onBeforeCompile) per mesh: procedural, UV-free marks evaluated from the vertex's
      rest-pose `position` (head-local for the head mesh, bind-pose body space for body /
      hands / feet), so marks stick to the skin in every pose and expression.
      Uniform budget: ≤ 24 point marks + ≤ 6 area layers per material; keep it WebGL1-safe.
   b. Layer kinds in this story (each with ≥ 5 options):
        freckles   – density, size, darkness; areas: nose & cheeks / whole face /
                     shoulders & arms (body mesh)
        mole       – point marks: size, raised (tiny normal bump), colour (brown/dark)
        beautySpot – single small dark point at preset spots (above lip L/R, cheekbone,
                     chin, under eye) or tap-placed
        birthmark  – soft irregular patch (café-au-lait / port-wine-stain / pale) at a
                     tapped point, size and opacity
        vitiligo   – depigmented patches (noise-thresholded, soft edges, slightly pink at
                     the border on deep skin): amount 0…1; areas: around eyes, around
                     mouth, hands, arms, whole body; symmetric option
        scar       – thin line (eyebrow slit scar, cheek, chin, lip) with a subtle normal
                     dip, colour lighter or darker than skin
        flush      – rosy cheeks / nose (rosacea-like) amount; under-eye tone (subtle
                     darker or bluish) amount
      Nothing is added by default (clear skin) — every layer is opt-in.
   c. Data: Look.skinMarks?: SkinMark[] (≤ 24), SkinMark = { k: kind; at?: [x, y, z] (local);
      space: 'head' | 'body'; size: number; amount: number; tone?: string; area?: string;
      sym?: boolean }. normalizeLook validates kinds, clamps numbers, drops unknown
      entries, caps the list.
   d. Marks render in thumbnails/captures and on every screen the avatar appears; when an
      expression or pose deforms the face/body, marks move with it.
   e. Hair, beards, eyebrows, makeup and clothes draw over the skin — marks never show
      through them.

4) TAP TO PLACE — AvatarStage.tsx + studio
   - `pickSurfacePoint(event) → { space, point(local), normal }`: raycast the head/body
     meshes (skinned: use the rest-pose position of the hit triangle's vertices so stored
     points are pose-independent).
   - In the Skin details panel: choose a mark, tap the face (camera already on 'head'
     stage) or body; long-press a mark to move, pinch/slider to resize, bin to delete;
     "Mirror" duplicates to the other side; undo/redo covers every action.

5) SELFIE → TONE & UNDERTONE — selfie/analyze.ts + generate.ts
   - From the already-sampled skin patches compute L*a*b*; depth = nearest L* step,
     undertone from hue angle / chroma (cool / neutral / warm / olive), plus `fine` to
     close the gap. Freckles: detect high-frequency dark spots on the cheeks/nose region
     and, if clearly present, OFFER (not force) a freckles layer on the result screen.
     All on-device; the photo never leaves the phone.

6) STUDIO UI — editor/categories.ts + AvatarStudio.tsx

     (Cool / Neutral / Warm / Olive) with a one-line hint, + "Fine tune" (hue & lightness
     sliders), + "Classic tones" to reach the old 100 swatches.
   - New "Skin details" category (Face & body tab): grid of mark kinds with thumbnails,
     per-kind controls (amount, size, area), tap-to-place hint, list of placed marks.
   - All new labels translated (en, hi, kn, ta, te) via tools/i18n TSV + build.
