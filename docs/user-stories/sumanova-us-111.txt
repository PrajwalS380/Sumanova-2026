=========================================================================================
sumanova-us-111 · A sculpted face, not a ball — real bone structure with cartoon charm
=========================================================================================

USER STORY
  As a SUMANOVA user, I want my avatar's face to have real structure — cheekbones, a brow
  ridge, soft temples, a jaw angle, a chin, the plane under the cheekbones and a gentle
  smile fold — so that faces look distinct and characterful from every angle instead of
  like a smooth ball with features drawn on, while staying friendly and cartoon-cute.

WHY (what build-103 has today)
  - The face is an analytic deformed sphere (sculpt.ts: deform / kxAt / skullDist /
    surfaceZ) with only width, height, depth, jaw taper (jawN, jawExp), cheek width
    (cheeks), chin drop (chinLow) and top width (topWide). sculpt3d/head.ts adds only two
    faint cheek "apples" kept deliberately inset — so every face reads round and flat,
    especially in profile and 3/4.
  - Why it was kept flat: eyes, brows, mouth, blush, nose shading, beards, glasses, hats,
    jewellery and the hair groom are all placed on the ANALYTIC surface (surfaceZ /
    shellGeometry / deform / skullDist — used in render/Face.tsx, render/Accessories.tsx,
    render/Jewelry.tsx, sculpt3d/groom.ts, sculpt3d/head.ts). Any bump added only to the
    sculpted head mesh makes those features sink in or float off. This story fixes that at
    the root instead of avoiding structure.

1) NEW BONE-STRUCTURE PARAMS — look/face.ts (HeadP, all optional, 0 … 1 unless noted)
     browRidge     prominence of the brow bone over the eyes
     cheekbone     prominence of the cheekbones (zygomatic arch)
     cheekHeight   −1 … 1 (low … high cheekbones)
     temple        softness of the temple hollow (0 full … 1 hollow)
     underCheek    the plane / soft hollow under the cheekbone
     jawAngle      definition and flare of the jaw corner (gonial angle)
     chinPad       forward projection of the chin
     chinWidth     −1 … 1 (pointed … broad chin)
     chinCleft     0 … 1 (none … visible cleft) — subtle
     smileFold     the soft fold from nose wing toward the mouth corner (nasolabial)
   a. Give each of the 10 head families (Oval, Round, Square, Heart, Long, Diamond, Pear,
      Soft, Wide, Narrow) a characteristic default set (e.g. Square: strong jawAngle and
      chinWidth; Heart: high cheekbones, narrow chin; Diamond: prominent cheekbones,
      narrow temples and chin; Soft/Round: low values everywhere) and let HEAD_STEP vary
      them a little per step so the 10 steps inside a family differ in structure too.
      Ids and family order never change.
   b. sculptV2.ts headFromParams(p, faceTune, fem = 0): add `fem` (0 masculine … 1 feminine,
      from the body variant's BodyP.fem). Masculine: stronger brow ridge, jaw angle and
      chin; feminine: higher, softer cheekbones, softer jaw, smaller brow ridge. Blend,
      don't switch. Figure.tsx and lookParts.ts pass variant(look,'body').p.fem. The
      HeadShape carries the final values (they are part of the head job key).

2) ONE SHARED FACE SURFACE — sculpt.ts  (the key change)
   a. Add `faceRelief(h, x, y, z?) → dz`: a small, smooth radial offset (head-local units)
      built from 8–10 soft gaussian/ellipsoidal lobes driven by the params above:
        brow ridge (positive band over the eyes), temples (negative, beside the eyes, under
        the hairline), cheekbones (positive ridge from below the outer eye toward the ear),
        under-cheek (negative plane below it), jaw angle (positive at the jaw corners),
        chin pad (positive, forward), chin cleft (narrow negative line), smile fold (a soft
        step along the nose-wing → mouth-corner curve).
      Limits: |dz| ≤ 0.014 at the strongest setting; gradient small enough that the
      skull field stays ≈ 1-Lipschitz (no mesher holes).
      Keep-out zones (relief fades to 0): the eye sockets' openings (eyes sit on them), a
      0.08-wide ellipse around the mouth (lips/teeth are drawn on the surface; anything
      bulging there hides them — see the comment in head.ts), the nose root, the ears.
   b. Use faceRelief EVERYWHERE the head surface is defined, so all callers agree:
        deform()      — radial multiplier includes relief (shellGeometry → beards, hats)
        skullDist()   — distance includes relief (sculpted head, hair groom, hair cap)
        surfaceZ()    — returned depth includes relief (eyes, brows, mouth, blush, nose
                        shading, glasses, jewellery)
      This guarantees features, beards, hats, glasses and hair follow the new structure.
      Remove the two ad-hoc cheek "soft" ellipsoids in head.ts (their role moves into the
      cheekbone lobe) unless they still help the cartoon look — decide by screenshot.
   c. Performance: faceRelief is evaluated very often (mesher, groom). Precompute the lobe
      centres/radii once per HeadShape (memoise by object identity / key) and early-out
      for points far behind the face (z < −0.2·rz) and above the hairline zone.

3) KEEP EVERYTHING ELSE FITTING
   - Beards (Face.tsx Beard / shellGeometry): jaw angle and chin now push the beard shell
     outward automatically through deform(); check stubble density still reads.
   - Hair (groom.ts uses skullDist / scalp / deform): temples and brow ridge must not make
     the hair cap or baby hairs (us-107) poke into the forehead; keepOff margins unchanged.
   - Glasses (Accessories.tsx): lenses use surfaceZ — check they don't clip into a strong
     brow ridge or cheekbones; adjust the frame z-offset by the relief at the eye if needed.
   - Ears / nose / earrings: placed relative to h.rx and surfaceZ — check jaw flare

   - DeepSet eye shadow decal (Face.tsx) should now sit naturally under the brow ridge;
     keep it, reduce its opacity if double-darkening.
